## Michael's notes, answered

### 1. Bart's reply to the day-2 case: "Can't make it all fit... Fits what I know"

**Verdict: the reply joins two pieces that contradict each other. It's a wording and
assembly problem, not a wrong belief (Herald).**

- **What Bart actually thought:** he accepted the case strongly. His belief that Merrin is
  evil went from 0.16 to 0.67 and his suspicion of her from 0.62 to 0.70
  (`explain_case --card 78 --listener bart`).
- **Why he also said it doesn't fit:** the case as filed answers Kiran's "3 evil among five"
  by counting Bart as one of the three. Bart knows he isn't evil, so his own quick check of
  the case (the bounded search) found no version of events where the case and what he knows
  both hold. It gave up without testing any.
- **Where the Bart answer came from:** the player picked him. A Dowser card's answer is set
  only by clicking a face on the card (`CanvasModel.apply_answer`); nothing fills it in
  automatically. The click itself isn't logged, but the answer was already on day 1's case
  #38 ("Bartholomew is evil"), and Nisha's reply to that case mentions "unproved slot
  choices". The answer stays on the card, so it carried into day 2's case #78 about Merrin.
  That case doesn't need Bart to be evil.
- **Possible UI point (Herald):** an answer picked for one case staying on the card for the
  next may be easy to miss. If it surprised Michael, the Workshop could show the carried
  answers when a new case is filed.
- **How the reply is built:** `render_workshop_reply` (`sim/dialogue_presentations.gd`,
  around line 317) leads with `present_clashes_own` for that result: "Doesn't square with
  what I've got. Can't make it all fit. Doesn't mean it's wrong." Then, because his stance
  is "accept", it adds `present_accept_open`, which begins "Fits what I know." That second
  line is written for a listener with no clash. The same pairing exists two branches up,
  after `present_conflict_weighing`.
- **Suggested fix:** one generic follow-on cue per voice for "part of this doesn't fit what
  I know, but it still moves me". Use it after either clash opener instead of
  `present_accept_open`. That is one line in each of the 15 voices, and nothing tied to a
  particular gift or card. Michael prefers this to replies that answer a specific role's
  reading, which would multiply across evidence types and voices. A draft for Bart, whose
  wording is Michael's call: *"Some of that doesn't square with what I've got. Still changes
  how I see Merrin. The street routes and traces lean your way. Circumstantial, mind."*

### 2. Merrin's defence: a home alibi, then "Then Kiran speaks with the serpent's own tongue!"

**Verdict: routing (Beadle), with a small wording follow-up (Herald). It's a gap in an
earlier fix.**

- **How the defence was built:** `defend_self` (`sim/mind_meeting.gd`) made two moves.
  - First, the case hinged on Merrin's night, so it opened "Your case rests on where I was
    last night" and gave her (false) home alibi.
  - Second, step 3 challenged the reading in the case, Kiran's Dowser count. Merrin's
    Confidence (0.72) chose the "lying" version, and every voice's version of that line
    starts with "Then".
- **The earlier fix (triage 2.2):** it already handles a "Then X is lying" that follows
  nothing. It rewrites the hinge line to "Your whole case rests on X's reading, and that
  reading is wrong". But that only applies when the hinge is the reading.
- **Why it didn't apply here:** the hinge was the night, so no line says the reading is
  wrong, and "Then Kiran is lying" comes straight after an alibi it doesn't follow from.
- **Suggested fix (Beadle):** when step 3 challenges a reading that isn't the hinge, first
  add the denial line ("And Kiran's reading is wrong"), or use a variant of the lying line
  without "Then".
- **Wording (Herald):** all 15 voices' `defence_lying` lines need a version that can stand
  on its own. Also, "They are lying" is in the present tense about Kiran, who died the night
  before. "Kiran lied" would fit.

## Working as intended

- **Bartholomew's watch said "saw Dorian", yet he also saw Merrin on the Square.** He was
  poisoned, so his gift lied while his own eyes didn't. Merrin was leaving the Square to
  hunt, and a Night Watch reading reports arrivals, not departures, so both lines can stand.
  The town can work it out: the clash between his two accounts points to the poison.
- **Lian's "I saw them out on Chapel Hill" against Bartholomew:** a true sighting (he left
  the Hill for his watch), used for the scapegoat plan. Evil picked Bartholomew as
  scapegoat on day 2 after his day-1 case.
