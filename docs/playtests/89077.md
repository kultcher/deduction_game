## Michael's notes, answered

### 1. Hollis against a Surveyor case: "Gift could've been turned"

**Verdict: routing (Beadle), plus a wording flag (Herald).**

- **The line:** seq 139. `Mind.defend_self`, step 3 ("against a reading that names them"),
  in `sim/mind_meeting.gd` (around 1750–1830). The full line was *"A simpler hypothesis is
  epistemic interference: Agnes's gift could well have been turned"*, which is Hollis's
  voice for `defence_misled`.
- **Why it was chosen:** step 3 has only three answers to a reading:
  - a role-specific hook (`try_reading_defence`), which only the Seer has;
  - `pair_other` ("one of the two, and it isn't me, so look at X"), which fires only when
    the reading's data has a two-person `pair`;
  - otherwise it challenges the reading: `lying` above 0.6 Confidence, `misled` below.
    Hollis has 0.38 Confidence, so he got `misled`.
- **Why that's the wrong kind of answer:** a Surveyor reading stores `{block, same}`, not a
  `pair`, so `pair_other` never fires for it. Here the block was in effect a pair. Chapel
  Hill had two residents, so "mixed" meant exactly one of Hollis and Dorian was evil.
  Hollis knew he was good. The `pair_other` test would also have passed: Hollis's suspicion
  of Dorian was 0.32 and of Agnes 0.11 (`replay_log --why` at the vote, seq 167).
- **Suggested fix (Beadle):** a general move rather than a Surveyor case. When a reading says
  "at least one of this group is evil" (a Seer "yes", a Surveyor "mixed" block, a count
  reading over a group) and the defender is in the group, "it's another of us" is available
  under the same test `pair_other` uses. With one other member it names them. With more it
  says "someone else on the block".
- **Wording (Herald):** "epistemic interference" and "hypothesis" sound modern next to
  DESIGN's "refined, not modern" rule. Hollis's and Nisha's voices use this register widely
  (about 40 lines across the voices use *datum, hypothesis, epistemic, empirical, taxonomy,
  premise*). Michael should say how far a scholarly voice may go.
- **Draft for the new move, in Hollis's voice (wording is Michael's call):** *"Agnes's
  reading says Chapel Hill is divided. I know my own allegiance, so the division lies with
  Dorian."* The plainer voices could adapt the existing pair line: *"Agnes said Chapel Hill
  isn't all of one side. It isn't me, so look at Dorian."*

### 2. Nisha, shown a vigil sighting, defends a sighting of her own

**Verdict: routing (Beadle). Michael is right that it's systemic: it happens whenever
someone is pressed with another person's night sighting of them and stands on a home
alibi. Wording follows from the fix (Herald).**

- **The line:** seq 275. The player presented #33, their own vigil card ("you saw Nisha on
  the Market"). Nisha (evil) chose to lie (seq 272–273), and `_answer_pressed_night`
  (`sim/mind_dialogue.gd`) produced the right answer: `pressed_home_stand`, *"I was at home
  last night. My account is unchanged."*
- **What went wrong:** the pressed-card path then calls `_challenged` (`sim/mind.gd:93`),
  which adds a "you doubt my account" opener to the first spoken line. It sets
  `visual = true` whenever the card is a sighting or vigil, or a Night Watch reading, and
  doesn't check whose sighting it is. With `visual` set, `Dialogue.compose` swaps in the
  voice's `doubted_visual` cue, *"Seeing a face isn't an inference; I made sure who I was
  looking at before I set it down"*. That line is written for a witness defending what they
  saw. All 15 voices have a `doubted_visual` cue, so every voice does this.
- **Suggested fix:** `visual` should need the card's `source` to be the speaker. Someone
  else's sighting of the speaker should get the plain opener or none. The code's own
  comment says "the opener only fits a restated sighting or alibi".
- **A second, smaller problem:** "My account is unchanged" (and the alternative, "I stand by
  that account") assumes the speaker gave an account before. Nisha hadn't been asked about
  that night. A first-time answer should be a plain "I was at home last night". Herald:
  the first-time and repeat versions of `pressed_home_stand`.
- **Also:** Nisha's plain `doubted` openers ("which part do you dispute: the person, the
  place, or the time?") also read oddly ahead of a denial. Herald should check whether any
  opener belongs there at all.

### 3. Constance's repeated clause, and a Lamplighter count used as a firm case

**Verdict: two problems. The repetition is routing (Beadle). The weight the count is given
is a belief-explanation problem (Lantern). The fallback sentence is wording (Herald).**

**The repetition.** At seq 780, Constance's case against Kiran was three items, one per
Lamplighter reading (the night before you arrived, the first night, the second night).
Each came out as the same sentence: "my reading, taken with the other reports, leaves them
among the likeliest" (contrib 0.23 each). `nomination_case` (`sim/mind_meeting.gd:1486`)
takes the first two public reasons and joins them with "; and". It removes only a reason
equal to the opening accusation, so two identical sentences went out. I reproduced this
exactly at seq 779 by calling `case_against` from a probe.
- **Fix (Beadle):** drop duplicate sentences in the recap. Better, merge readings from the
  same gift into one reason ("my counts on three nights...").
- **Wording ("established record"):** that is Constance's voice for `nomination_recap`, and
  "the established record" oversells a recap of one's own claims. Herald, low priority.

**The weight given to the count.** A Lamplighter count ("I counted 3 others out") names
nobody. `_reading_gist` (`workshop/reason_attribution.gd:1341`) finds nobody it is about
and returns nothing. So the item falls to the generic sentence "leaves them among the
likeliest" (`reason_attribution.gd:918`), whatever the reading actually says about the
person.
- **What I measured:** a probe on Constance's good-self mind (the one an evil nominator's
  reasons come from) at seq 779.
  - Her suspicion of Kiran was 0.36, her highest, so "among the likeliest" was literally
    true.
  - Removing all three Lamplighter readings and her Lamplighter claim together lowered it
    only to 0.31. Removing any one alone moved it by less than 0.005.
  - Yet the attribution credits each reading separately with contrib 0.23 and strength
    0.72.
  - The same happened against Nisha: three identical reasons at 0.41 each, while removing
    the readings lowered her good-self's suspicion of Nisha only from 0.24 to 0.17.
- **Michael's point holds.** A count of people out can't separate Kiran, who claims a gift
  that doesn't walk, from anyone else who says they were home. It weighs on every
  non-walker alike. The good minds agree: the readings don't show up among the top things
  moving any good mind's suspicion of Kiran (`explain_case --belief kiran --at 779`).
- **Possible cause (not verified):** the leave-one-out in part B of the attribution
  re-checks role fits (`_loo_post`, `needs_fits_recheck`) whenever another card shares the
  same role. That's true for each of her three Lamplighter readings, which may be why each
  gets the identical drop.
- **For Lantern:** check why a reading whose removal barely moves suspicion is reported as
  a strong reason, and whether three readings from one gift should each be credited with
  the whole joint drop.
- **Draft fallback wording for a count that names nobody (Michael's call):** *"my count of
  folk abroad comes to more than have owned to being out"*. That is honest about what a
  count can show, and it doesn't single out anyone.

## Other findings

1. **Bug: Dorian's defence argues both ways (Beadle).**
   - **What happened:** at seq 448 Dorian said "I don't remember going out... I've been
     known to sleepwalk", then "that reading is wrong", then "couldn't Edda's gift have been
     turned?"
   - **Why:** step 2b (sleepwalk) accepts the sighting and step 3 rejects it, and step 3
     doesn't exclude `sleepwalk`. The code already rules out the same clash for
     `pair_other` ("never both").
   - **Fix:** a one-line exclusion in `defend_self`.
2. **Bug: Fenwick, the publicly claimed Warden accused of being out, pleads desperation
   (Beadle).**
   - **What happened:** at seq 761 Nisha nominated Fenwick: "I saw them out in the street
     from my window last night". Fenwick answered "I don't know what's going on, but I know
     I'm not evil".
   - **Why:** `defend_self` builds what it answers from the nominator's reasons
     (`_disclosed_items_against`). Nisha's good-self doesn't suspect Fenwick: this was a
     scapegoat push, and her log says "no citable reasons on record". So the list was empty
     (confirmed with a probe at seq 759), and none of the gift, secret, sleepwalk or reading
     defences could fire.
   - **What it should be:** "I'm the Warden; I was at Jory's door". He had claimed Warden in
     public that same evening.
   - **Fix:** the defence should also answer the accusation actually spoken (the sighting
     in `already`).
3. **Bug: Nisha's nomination contradicts itself (Beadle).**
   - **What happened:** at seq 761 she said "I saw them out in the street from my window
     last night", then "I don't have a public premise to put on the table yet, though
     Fenwick is my top suspect".
   - **Why:** `nomination_case` adds `nomination_no_reasons` whenever the reason list is
     empty, even when the opening accusation was a sighting. This has the same root as
     finding 2.
   - Constance's version at seq 491 ("Though I cannot lay every ground before the hall
     tonight") reads acceptably, so only some voices' lines clash.
4. **Bug: Constance calls Kiran a liar over a reading the case doesn't use (Beadle).**
   - **What happened:** at seq 714, against the player's case #127 (the player's vigil,
     Fenwick's claim, and Fenwick's sighting of her), Constance said "If Kiran makes that
     claim against me, then Kiran is lying".
   - **Why:** for a player nomination, `defend_self` picks as the accuser the NPC who
     suspects the defender most, here Kiran. Step 3 then challenges the first reading in
     that NPC's reasons, Kiran's Psychologist reading (probe at seq 711: Kiran is the only
     source, and the item isn't in the case). That reading is about Dorian's and Edda's
     statements, not Constance.
   - **Caveat:** step 3's comment says that against a filed case it should go after the
     reading the case rests on. Here the case rests on no reading, so a reading challenge
     shouldn't be offered at all.
5. **Bug (wording): the Warden says "Night 1", not "the first night" (Herald).**
   - **What happened:** at seq 566 Fenwick said "Night 1 at Agnes's door; night 2 at Jory's
     door".
   - **Why:** `warden.gd:134` (`answer_gift_custom`) uses the card-text `night_name` in
     speech. Spoken lines should use `spoken_night` (DESIGN: nights are spoken as
     ordinals).
   - Herald may want to check the other `night_name_fn` users in spoken lines.
6. **Bug (wording): "The night before you arrived, My register lists three roles" (Herald).**
   - **What happened:** seq 242 and 405, Merrin.
   - **Why:** the Registrar's reading text starts with a capital letter and is in the
     present tense, and it gets glued after the night phrase.
7. **Bug, low: the end-of-case evidence counts readings held by the dead (Quill).**
   - **What happened:** `case_metrics.demon_evidence` listed "Edda's nightwatch reading
     (night 2)" as reachable. Edda learned it just before Constance killed her, and no
     Medium was alive to hear it.
   - **Why:** the docstring says "held by living townsfolk", but the loop doesn't check
     `alive`.
   - **Effect:** the town won, so the player never saw it. It does feed the end screen on a
     loss, and the `loss_hint` in sweeps.
