# Reading interpretation: how each gift's readings should be read (design note)

**Status: built gift by gift with Michael, started Nov 21, 2026. Nothing here is a change
yet.** Written by Claude-Vicar.
- **What it is:** for each gift, how its readings should be interpreted, by the reader and by
  each person they name. It also covers how the reader picks targets and when it hides or
  reveals.
- **What happens next:** after each gift is written, the leads compare it with what's modelled
  today (Lantern for the judge and beliefs, Quill for evidence, Beadle for behaviour). They
  note gaps both ways: behaviour described here and not modelled, and behaviour modelled but
  not recorded here. Then the two are reconciled.
- **Evil's side** (how to bluff each gift) is left out until that behaviour is worked on
  (Michael, Nov 21). It feeds the "Smarter evil" and "Good NPCs lie tactically" work.

---

## 1. The ground everyone reasons from

### 1.1 What everyone knows about the game

- **The counts:** the exact numbers of good and evil, of Demons and Minions, and, for now, of
  burdens. Later, possibly only the expected number of burdens.
- **The script:** which roles could be in play, and which burdens are possible.
- **That bluffs exist:** evil is given good roles that aren't in play to claim. This is
  today's rule, and it may change.
- **Who is alive and who is dead.**
- **Whether a Demon is alive:** always true while the game goes on. The information is in a
  hanging the game survives: either the hanged person wasn't the Demon, or a Scion took over
  (with five or more alive).
- **How deaths read:** a Demon victim starts at 95:5 odds of being good, rather than a flat
  95%, so a victim the town already suspected comes out lower (Lantern's check, Nov 21). It's
  strong but not certain, since a Demon may one day kill its own Minion.
- **What was said:** every public claim, accusation and case, and what was said to you in
  private. That it was said is certain; whether it's true isn't.
- **The player's public acts:** nominations, votes and presented cases.
- **Public special events:**
  - a Martyr's sacrifice proves both people good;
  - a quiet night means someone was protected or the Demon held back;
  - kill-scene traces.

### 1.2 What everyone knows about themselves

- **Their own role, alignment and known burdens,** and a known burden's own facts (the
  Unrequited knows their beloved).
- **Their home, work, work schedule and haunts.**
- **Their own whereabouts at all times.** This holds fully now that the old Sleepwalker is
  retired.
- **Their own memory:** what they said, to whom, and whether they've claimed.
- **Their feelings:** trust, suspicion and grievances toward each person.
- **Their dealings with the player:** promises made and whether they were kept, wards and
  clemency.
- **The exception:** a Delusional person doesn't know it. To them, their own readings look
  like anyone's.

### 1.3 Firsthand and always true

- **Daytime sightings** of others.
- **Night sightings:** a walker is seen 50% of the time by someone out on its route, and 15%
  of the time from a home window on it.
  - **Open, for Michael** (Lantern's check, Nov 21): today, "not seen out" clears no one. The
    judge doesn't use the sim's sighting chances; only complete watch lists and coverage say
    someone wasn't out. Should the absence of a sighting count for anything?
- **Walking every block** (Michael, Nov 21): the Lamplighter walks every block every night, and
  the Sexton does on the night it tolls. A night sighting of either, on any block, on a night
  they walk, can't be wrong because of where it was.
- **The witness alibi:** a neighbour at home sometimes notices someone stayed in (20%). It's
  weak, but true.
- **The vote record:** who was nominated, who voted, and how many votes put someone on the
  block or clear it. By design, the judge gives votes no weight on alignment (Lantern's
  check, Nov 21).
- **Silencing:** who was silenced, and so that a Breathstealer is in play.
- **The Revenant's curse:** that it happened, and so that the person just hanged was the
  Revenant and evil, and that someone has been turned. Nobody knows who, so it's "one more
  evil", not a named person.
- **The player's promises:** whether the player kept their promise to them.
- **The Veteran's attack:** a Veteran knows it was attacked, and the block the attacker came
  through.
- **Traces found when searching.**

### 1.4 Firsthand but not always true

- **Their own gift's readings:** they may be poisoned or Delusional.
- **A Warden's "I made the save":** it's an inference, not something seen.

### 1.5 What can't be known for certain

- **Whether you were poisoned** at any given time. Poison comes from the Poisoner, and also
  from a hanged Ill-Omened holder (Quill's check, Nov 21).
- **How many evil are alive.** The bounds are known: the starting count, plus anyone turned by
  the curse. Which of the dead were evil isn't.
- **Anyone else's role or alignment,** short of proof (a Martyr's sacrifice, the curse).
- **Whether you're the Red Herring,** or Marked and misregistered in a particular reading.
- **Whether an unclaimed burden holder exists,** and who it is.
- **Whether you were visited by the Confessor:** the visited person never knows (Michael,
  Nov 21).
- **Whether the Sexton's bell held, for good:** never certain under any circumstances (Michael,
  Nov 21). A bluffing evil can claim to have rung it while nothing happens.

### 1.6 What evil knows for certain

- **Who the Demon is,** its own role, and who is on each team. The turned know the team, and
  the team knows them.
- **Its bluffs.** Today a bluff role is out of play, so a good claim of it is a lie unless the
  claimant is a teammate. The bluff rule may change.
- **Its own actions:** whom the Poisoner poisoned (and so which readings are false that night),
  whom the Demon attacked, and where the totem is.
- **The team's plan:** the scapegoat, and what was agreed at evil meetings.
- **When its abilities fail in an obvious way** (Michael, Nov 21): a Revenant's curse that
  doesn't happen, or a silencing that doesn't land. That can tell it the Sexton's bell held,
  or that its Minion was blocked.
- **What evil does *not* know:**
  - **everyone's roles** (Michael, Nov 21). It can be fairly confident in the claims of people
    it knows are good, since good lies sparingly;
  - who holds a burden;
  - who the Red Herring is;
  - whether a reading against a teammate came from Marked or from chance.

## 2. The gifts

Each gift gets:
- **what the reading does** and what the reader knows about its rules;
- **how the reader reads it,** which is also how someone who trusts the reader should read it;
- **how each person named reads it;**
- **target choice,** where the gift picks;
- **hiding and revealing;**
- **reading with other readings** (added by Michael, Nov 21): with the gift's own other
  readings, and with other gifts' readings.

**The strength words** (strong, moderate, mild) are Michael's intuition for now. They should be
checked against the real probabilities, landing somewhere between human intuition, game theory
and the actual odds (Michael, Nov 21).

The order (Michael, Nov 21):
1. the reading gifts that name people: Seer, Dowser, Surveyor, Augur, Confessor, Registrar,
   Clerk;
2. the gifts that start out knowing something: Archivist, Rumormonger, Chronicler, Executor;
3. the night watchers: Night Watch, Lamplighter, Monitor;
4. the dead: Gravedigger, Medium;
5. the Magistrate, the Psychologist, and the protective gifts.

### 2.1 The Seer (Michael, Nov 21)

**What the reading does.** Each night the Seer chooses two people. If one of them registers as
the Demon (not a Minion), it learns "One of those two people is the Demon"; otherwise "Neither
of those two people is the Demon". One randomly chosen good person, the Red Herring, registers
as the Demon to this ability.

**The Red Herring** is drawn once, at the deal, from the good people other than the Seer, and
stays the same all game (as built; Quill's check, Nov 21).

**What the Seer knows about its rules:**
- the Red Herring exists, but not who it is;
- Marked can make a good person register as the Demon, half the time, but only if Marked is
  on the script;
- the Scion doesn't register as the Demon until its ability has triggered.

**How the Seer reads its own readings** (and how someone who trusts the Seer should):
- **A "yes" on A and B:**
  - A is the Demon, or B is;
  - one of them is the Red Herring;
  - one of them is Marked and misregistered;
  - the Seer was poisoned that night, or is Delusional;
  - either or both could have triggered it: the Demon and the Red Herring can be in the same
    pick.
- **A "no" on A and B:**
  - neither A nor B is the Demon;
  - neither is the Red Herring;
  - if either is Marked, they didn't misregister;
  - one or both may have registered a "yes" normally but didn't, because the Seer was poisoned
    or is Delusional.

**How a person named reads it.** A, good and not Marked, named in a "yes", has these
explanations:
1. B is the Demon;
2. B is the Red Herring;
3. B is Marked and misregistered;
4. I'm the Red Herring;
5. the Seer is poisoned or Delusional;
6. the Seer is an evil bluffer, lying.
7. If A is Marked, A may have misregistered, though B may still be the Demon or the Red
   Herring.

**The player's side of a "yes"** (as built): the Workshop asks which explanation the player is
arguing (the Demon, the Red Herring or Marked, and who), and the case is graded on that choice.

*A named in a "no"* (confirmed by Michael, Nov 21; it matches the judge, per Lantern's check): B isn't the Demon, and I'm not the Red
Herring, unless the Seer is poisoned, Delusional or a lying bluffer.

**Target choice:**
- **Night 0:** essentially random.
- **Later nights:** the people the Seer most suspects of being the Demon, not just of being
  evil, such as people spotted out at night.
- **After a "yes":** check again on later nights, pairing one of the "yes" targets, 50/50, with
  someone fresh or with someone it trusts as good, to narrow down which one triggered it
  (Michael, Nov 21). This is gated by Insight today, which is fine.

**Hiding and revealing:**
- **Aim to hide until it has at least two readings.** The Seer is a major threat to the Demon,
  more so as the town shrinks, and a prime target once revealed. But waiting too long risks
  being killed at random before its information is out. The average reveal is around day 2.5.
- **Seed information without revealing:** it may find someone it trusts and pass its
  information through them. Hearsay has limited weight, but it can point people the right way.
- **Seek protection before revealing:** when it plans to reveal, it may look for protection
  from the player or a known Warden.

The last two are on the backlog (Michael, Nov 21).

**As built, not otherwise recorded here** (Beadle's check, Nov 21; 80 passive 10-NPC games):
- **Player requests:** the Seer can be asked to look at someone. It agrees or refuses by its
  trust in the player, whether it has already looked, and whether it would be doubting a friend.
  When it refuses, it names who it would rather look at.
- **Dull Seers scatter:** a focused look happens 30% of the time plus half the Seer's Insight.
  Otherwise it looks at two random people it hasn't paired before.
- **Settled after two yeses:** a sharp Seer whose top suspect has been in two "yes" readings
  stops re-checking them and looks at two fresh people.
- **No repeats:** it never repeats a pair.
- **Reactive reveals:** most reveals are triggered at the meeting, not timed. The triggers are
  someone else claiming Seer, the Seer being named in an accusation, or the Seer holding a
  reading that clears someone accused. In 40 baseline games, 12 of 18 Seer claims were reactive.
- **Today's timing:** 36 of 42 good Seers claimed, at a mean of day 2.1. Six held one reading
  when they claimed, 20 held two and 10 held three. Six died on day 2 or 3 unclaimed. The timed
  part is a calendar-day taper shared by every gift that keeps giving readings; nothing counts
  readings or the town's size.
- **Defences when named:** after one shared "yes", the nominee points at the other name ("One
  of the two, X said. It isn't me, so look at Y."). After two, it uses the Red Herring defence
  ("…keeps finding me because I'm the one the Seer always misreads"). That threshold dates from
  the Oct 19 meeting-flow work.

**Reading with other readings** (Michael, Nov 21):
- **With its own readings:** two "yes" readings that both include A strongly suggest that A is
  the Demon, the Red Herring or Marked. They moderately suggest the Seer isn't Delusional. The
  maths of the second is uncertain, but it holds up psychologically.
- **With readings that show alignment:**
  - an evil reading that overlaps a person in the "yes" moderately suggests that person is the
    Demon or Marked;
  - a good reading on one person in the pair moderately suggests the other is the Demon or
    Marked;
  - if both people in the pair are cleared, it mildly suggests one of: the Seer's reading was
    tampered with, the other reading's source is an evil bluffer, or the "yes" came from a
    Marked person who misregistered for the Seer but not for the other gift.
- **With night sightings:** a "yes" on someone sighted out at night, with no Hexer on the
  script, mildly suggests that person is the Demon. It's stronger if they haven't claimed a
  role that walks at night.

### 2.2 The Dowser (Michael, Nov 21)

**What the reading does.** The first time each day the Dowser is out in public on a block where
two or more others are also out, it learns how many of those people are evil. It's a daytime
reading, shared with the time it was taken.

**What the Dowser knows about its rules:**
- a Marked good person can register as evil, half the time, but only if Marked is on the script;
- there's no Red Herring;
- a turned person counts as evil.

**How the Dowser reads its own reading,** say "1 of A, B, C":
- one of the three is evil;
- or a Marked good person among them registered as evil;
- or it was poisoned, or is Delusional, and the true count differs.
- **A "0"** clears all three hard. It can only be wrong through poison, Delusion, or a Marked
  person among them happening to register as good. *This may be too strong in play; to watch
  (Michael, Nov 21).*

**How a person named reads it.** A, good and not Marked, in "1 of A, B, C":
1. B or C is evil;
2. B or C is Marked and misregistered;
3. the Dowser is poisoned or Delusional;
4. the Dowser is an evil bluffer who made up the count;
5. the Dowser is an evil bluffer who used a real gathering to frame A;
6. if A is Marked, A may have misregistered.

**Target choice:** none. There's no manual targeting: the reading is wherever the Dowser's day
takes it. It doesn't steer toward crowds, and the player can't ask it to look anywhere
(confirmed in Beadle's check, Nov 21).

**Hiding and revealing:** still cautious, but a little earlier than the Seer, around day 2 on
average. *Deferred (Michael, Nov 21):* the gap against today's timing waits for an overhaul of
reveal timing across all roles at once.
- **As built** (Beadle's check, Nov 21): the Dowser uses the same calendar-day taper as the
  Seer. 18 of 24 good Dowsers claimed, at a mean of day 2.3, later than the Seer's 2.1,
  probably because some days give it no crowd to read. Six died on day 1 unclaimed.

**Reading with other readings:**
- **With its own readings:**
  - the same person in several readings of 1 or more moderately suggests that person is evil or
    Marked;
  - the same person in several readings of 0 strongly suggests that person is good.
- **With other gifts' evil, Minion or Demon readings:** these moderately suggest the person
  named is what that reading says, and that the Dowser isn't poisoned or Delusional.
- **With other signs of good:** a stronger clearing, and support for the Dowser not being
  Delusional.

### 2.3 The Surveyor (Michael, Nov 21)

**What the reading does.** The Surveyor starts knowing whether all the residents of one random
block share the same alignment. The block is never its own, and always has at least two
residents. The answer is "same" or "mixed".

**What the Surveyor knows about its rules:**
- a Marked good resident can register as evil, half the time, if Marked is on the script;
- there's no Red Herring;
- the block is random, not chosen;
- it's a reading from before the game starts, so nobody has been turned.
- **Poison applies:** the Poisoner acts on night 0, before anyone learns anything. Verified in
  `sim/world_night.gd`: the night-0 poison lands before that night's readings are received.

**How the Surveyor reads its own reading:**
- **"Same":** all good or all evil. All evil is plausible only on a two-person block, and
  unlikely even there. On three or more residents it's close to a hard clear of the block. It
  also means no Marked resident misregistered.
- **"Mixed":** at least one resident is evil, or a Marked good resident registered as evil. On
  a two-person block it's sharp: one of the two.
- **Either answer** can be wrong if the Surveyor was poisoned on night 0 or is Delusional.

**How a resident reads it.** R, good and not Marked:
- **On "same":** the others on my block are good too, unless the Surveyor is poisoned,
  Delusional or an evil bluffer, lying.
- **On "mixed":**
  1. another resident is evil;
  2. another resident is Marked and misregistered;
  3. the Surveyor is poisoned or Delusional;
  4. the Surveyor is an evil bluffer, lying.

  On a two-person block, 1 and 2 point straight at the other resident.
- **If R is Marked:** on "mixed", R may be the cause.

**Target choice:** none.

**Hiding and revealing:** it holds one reading, and once that's out it has nothing more for the
Demon to fear. It's likely to reveal on day 1 (Michael, Nov 21). "Same" clears people, and
"mixed" on a small block is close to an accusation.

**Reading with other readings:**
- **With its own readings:** there are none.
- **With night sightings:** a resident of a "mixed" block seen out at night is moderate
  evidence that they're the evil one, if they've claimed a role that doesn't walk at night or
  haven't claimed at all. Otherwise it's weak (Michael, Nov 21).
- **With alignment readings on residents:**
  - a good reading on one resident of a two-person "mixed" block strongly points at the other;
  - an evil reading on a resident of a "same" block means one of the readings is wrong or a
    lie: the Surveyor or the other reader may be poisoned, Delusional, or an evil bluffer.
- **With the Dowser** (Michael, Nov 21):
  - a Dowser clear on a resident is moderate support that the resident is good;
  - a Dowser ping that includes a resident of a "mixed" block is weak support that they're one
    of the evil ones on that block.

## 3. Open questions for the cross-check

- **Agreement lowering self-doubt** (Michael, Nov 21): the more a reader's readings line up
  with the town's other readings, the less it should think its own readings were poisoned or
  Delusional. Today the judge recomputes each own reading's chance of having failed at every
  refresh, from everything the NPC holds, starting from the poison and Delusion prior
  (`docs/belief_model.md`, "Was my gift turned?"). A reading that fits the rest already falls
  below the prior. Two things may be missing:
  - Poison is per night, so agreement on one night says little about another. Delusion is for
    the whole game, so agreement on several nights should lower the Delusional doubt for all of
    them. Whether the judge pools that across nights is for Lantern to check.
  - Nothing is remembered: confidence built up on earlier days isn't kept once the evidence
    behind it is forgotten or contested. That's the tentative "Persistence Confidence" item in
    BACKLOG section 6.
- **From Beadle's check (Nov 21), for Michael:**
  - **The Seer's later picks** rank people by how evil it thinks they are, not by how likely
    each is to be the Demon. That's a bug against this note; a fix needs a Demon-specific belief
    from Lantern.
  - **Narrowing after a "yes"** re-checks the Seer's current top suspect with someone unknown,
    which doesn't isolate who triggered it. That's a bug against this note.
  - **The Seer's reveal** averages day 2.1 against the note's 2.5. "At least two readings"
    mostly holds already; whether to move the average later is Michael's choice.
  - **The Dowser's reveal** averages day 2.3, later than the Seer, against the note's "a little
    earlier". It needs a Dowser-specific claim term, a new constant, or a retune of the shared
    taper, which moves every reading gift.
  - **A "no" is final to the Seer itself:** it marks both people cleared outright, where the note
    leaves room for poison or Delusion. Lantern and Beadle are to reconcile it.
  - **The Red Herring defence after two yeses:** Beadle recommends keeping the threshold. If it
    were available from the first yes, it would need a different line, since this one says
    "keeps".
  - **Unverified:** whether evil reasons that a good claim of one of its bluff roles is a lie
    (section 1.6).
- **Michael's answers (Nov 21):**
  - **The Seer's picks:** the most likely Demon, as the note says; Beadle is fixing the code, and
    Lantern is adding per-person Demon odds.
  - **Narrowing after a "yes":** a "yes" target paired 50/50 with someone fresh or someone
    trusted; Beadle is fixing the code.
  - **The Red Herring defence** stays as built, after two shared yeses.
  - **The Dowser's reveal timing** is deferred to an overhaul of reveal timing across roles.
  - **"Not being seen out" as evidence:** on the backlog, under the circumstantial hub.
  - **Still with Michael:** the strength wording ("good, and not the Red Herring"; two Dowser
    readings of 1) and how Delusion's game-long doubt is shared across nights.
