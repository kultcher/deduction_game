## The readings problem: what's left (probe, Oct 30)

Seed 37291 with ten townsfolk, the player idle, at Day 3. Jory is the Seer, his red herring is Odette, and Fenwick is the Demon. Jory's readings:
- Kiran or Fenwick: yes;
- Odette or Fenwick: yes;
- Agnes or Edda: no.

What the shared model gives Jory (P(evil) before and after his evidence):

| Person | Before | After | Change to suspicion |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Agnes (evil) | 0.60 | 0.73 | +0.14 |
| Edda | 0.60 | 0.39 | −0.15 |
| Greta | 0.60 | 0.54 | −0.04 |
| Isolde (evil) | 0.61 | 0.55 | −0.04 |
| Kiran | 0.12 | 0.30 | +0.09 |
| Fenwick (Demon) | 0.12 | 0.18 | +0.03 |
| Odette, Constance, Merrin | about 0.12 | 0.06 to 0.14 | small |

Fenwick, in both "yes" pairs, ends at a suspicion of 0.02. A clean count of the possible red herrings puts P(Fenwick is the Demon) alone at about 0.37.

**Cause: the starting estimate swamps the readings.**
- `CaseEval._team_prior` reads each person's suspicion as a probability. It clamps 0 up to 0.02, then multiplies by an npc_trust factor.
- Jory's outside suspicion was 0.1 for four people and 0.0 for the rest. That turns into about a 5× difference in prior odds, before any evidence.
- One Seer "yes" is worth about 3×. So the readings are counted, but they're outweighed by near-zero initial suspicion values that were never meant as evidence.

**Smaller factors:**
- The update is scaled by `(0.5 + trust in the player)`, even for the NPC's own evidence. Jory's trust in the player was 0.40, giving a factor of 0.9.
- `CaseEval.shift` is small at low probabilities.

**Options to discuss:**
- flatten the prior (map suspicion into a narrow odds band, or take the base rate from the setup and only nudge it by suspicion);
- don't scale an NPC's own readings by trust in the player;
- add a regression test on this seed.
